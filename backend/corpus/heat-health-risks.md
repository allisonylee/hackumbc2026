**Key takeaway:** Extreme heat can make anyone sick, but CDC says some groups face higher risk: adults 65 and older, infants and young children, pregnant people, people with asthma or heart disease, and outdoor workers and athletes. Staying cool, drinking water and checking on others are the main ways to stay safe.

## Warning signs of overheating
CDC lists symptoms that can mean your body is getting too hot:
- muscle cramps
- unusually heavy sweating
- shortness of breath
- dizziness
- headache
- weakness
- nausea

Know these symptoms, and know when to seek medical care.

## Who is most at risk
- **Older adults (65+)**
- **Infants and young children**
- **Pregnant people**
- **People with asthma or heart disease**
- **Outdoor workers and athletes**

CDC notes that anyone can get sick from heat, but these groups may need to take extra steps on hot days.

## How to protect yourself
- Stay in the shade when you are outside.
- Use air conditioning if you have it, or go to a cooling center or other air-conditioned place.
- Fans can help, but CDC advises relying on them only when indoor temperatures are below **90°F**. Above 90°F, a fan can actually raise body temperature.
- Drink plenty of water.
- Check the HeatRisk forecast to know when heat is dangerous.
- If you take medicines, ask your doctor whether they affect how your body handles heat, and make a plan.
- Check on neighbors, family and friends who are at higher risk.

## Why trees matter here
Shade from street trees lowers the heat people are exposed to on sidewalks, stoops and bus stops, which is where many high-risk residents spend time outdoors.
