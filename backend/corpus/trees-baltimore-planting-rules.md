**Key takeaway:** In Baltimore you need Forestry Division permission before planting in the public right-of-way. At least half the trees in any project must be native, no species may be more than 10 percent of a project of over 20 trees, street trees must be at least 1½-inch caliper, and every planting needs a two-year care plan. Weekly summer watering matters most.

## The city's goal
Baltimore aims for **40 percent tree canopy cover**. The city prefers larger trees wherever possible, because large shade trees give more benefits and live longer than small understory trees.

## Diversity and native species
- **Native share:** at least half of the trees installed in each planting project must be native. Native species are better adapted to local conditions and give more ecological benefits.
- **Seed source:** trees should come from as local a seed source as possible, ideally within 200 miles of Baltimore City.
- **Species cap:** for projects of more than 20 trees, no single species should be more than 10 percent of the total. A diverse urban forest is more resilient to pests and disease.
- **Right tree, right place:** check sunlight, soil volume and quality, water sources and other conditions at the site.

## Planting requirements
- **Permission** from the Forestry Division is required before planting any tree along streets in the public right-of-way (ROW) or on city parkland (see treebaltimore.org/tree-order).
- **Size:** trees along streets, busy sidewalks or next to playgrounds must be at least **1½-inch caliper** (trunk diameter measured 6 inches above the ground). Park trees must be at least ½-inch caliper.
- **Nursery quality:** trees must meet the American Standard for Nursery Stock (ANSI Z60.2) and have a single dominant leader.
- **Tree pit size, soil and planting details** follow the Baltimore City Landscape Manual.

## Aftercare
A **two-year maintenance plan is required** for all plantings. It covers routine watering, mulching, weeding and trunk protection. The city calls weekly summer watering the most important factor in whether a new tree survives.

## Size classes and locations used on the list
Small is under 35 feet, medium is 35–50 feet, and large is over 50 feet. Small trees suit narrow streets, small pits and spots under overhead wires. Medians count only if they are at least 15 feet wide. Some species are approved only for parks, and some are banned everywhere (for example Norway maple, Callery pear, tree of heaven and all ash species, which are blighted by emerald ash borer).
