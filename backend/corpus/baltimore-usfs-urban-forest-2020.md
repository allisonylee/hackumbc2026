**Key takeaway:** A U.S. Forest Service study estimates Baltimore has about **1.9 million live trees**, and that they store large amounts of carbon, clean the air, and save millions of dollars in energy costs every year.

## About the study
"Baltimore's urban forest, 2020" (Resource Bulletin NRS-139) was written by Nancy Falxa Sonti, Dexter Locke and colleagues and published by the USDA Forest Service in 2025. It estimated trees across all ownerships in the city. The full PDF is at https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/69884.pdf.

## Key findings
- **Number of trees:** about 1.9 million live trees across all ownerships.
- **Forest land:** 39.2% of those trees (about 731,696 trees) grow on forest land.
- **Most common species:** tree of heaven, northern white-cedar, American elm, green ash and Norway maple. (Tree of heaven and Norway maple are non-native species.)

## Benefits
- **Carbon stored:** about 440,635 tons of carbon (1.6 million tons of carbon dioxide), valued at $75.2 million.
- **Carbon removed each year:** about 15,200 tons of carbon (56,000 tons of carbon dioxide) per year, valued at $2.6 million per year.
- **Air pollution removed:** about 347 tons per year, valued at $11.8 million per year.
- **Energy savings:** trees cut annual energy costs of residential buildings by about $7.2 million per year.
- **Total value:** the cost to replace the forest (its compensatory value) is estimated at $2.28 billion.

## Why it matters
These numbers show that trees are city infrastructure. Each new tree adds to shade, cleaner air and carbon storage as it grows.
