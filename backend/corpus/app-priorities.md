---
title: "What the 'who benefits' sliders and equity guarantee do"
url: https://github.com/allisonylee/hackumbc2026
source: A Tree Grows in Baltimore (hackUMBC 2026)
---

The Plan tab's sliders change which blocks get trees first. They set how much weight goes to heat, equity, health and ecology.

**Heat** ranks sites by how much cooling they bring to how many residents, per dollar. **Equity** gives extra weight to blocks with more poverty, lower incomes and more people of color. **Health** gives extra weight to blocks with higher asthma rates and higher social vulnerability. These two scale the cooling rather than replace it: a block where a tree would cool nobody gets nothing from them, so every plan still puts trees where they actually cool people. **Ecology** adds a per-tree credit for carbon, stormwater and air quality that ignores heat and people, so raising it favors cheap, large, long-lived trees wherever they fit.

Only the balance between sliders matters, not their absolute level. The sliders make the biggest difference at small and medium budgets. With a large budget, the best blocks fill up and plans with different priorities end up choosing many of the same sites.

The **equity guarantee** is a hard rule rather than a preference: it reserves at least a chosen share of the trees for lower-income blocks, even if that costs some total cooling. A chart in the Plan tab shows this trade-off between total cooling and the share going to low-income blocks.

You can also exclude neighborhoods and avoid sites under overhead power lines.
