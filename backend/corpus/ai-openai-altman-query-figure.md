**Key takeaway:** OpenAI CEO Sam Altman wrote in 2025 that the average ChatGPT query uses about **0.34 Wh** of energy and about **0.000085 gallons** of water. This is a company-reported number with no stated method.

## What he said
In his blog post "The Gentle Singularity", Altman gave these figures and compared them to everyday things:
- The energy is about what an oven uses in a little over one second, or an efficient light bulb in a couple of minutes.
- The water is about one fifteenth of a teaspoon.

He also wrote that the cost of intelligence should eventually approach the cost of electricity.

## Why to treat it with care
- **Company-reported.** The number comes from the company that runs the service.
- **No method given.** The post does not say which model, what counts as a query, or whether cooling, idle servers or other data center overhead are included.
- **Not independently checked.** Outside researchers cannot reproduce it from the post alone.
- **An average.** Long conversations, big file uploads, image generation, or reasoning models likely use more.

## How it compares
The figure is close to two other 2025 numbers:
- Epoch AI's outside estimate of about 0.3 Wh for a typical GPT-4o query.
- Google's measured median of about 0.24 Wh for a Gemini text prompt.

Because these three sources roughly agree, a fair summary is that a typical text chatbot query in 2025 used well under 1 Wh. That is small for one person, but the total grows with billions of queries each day.
