---
title: "How big does a street tree pit need to be in Baltimore? Minimum tree pit size and soil volume"
url: https://www.baltimoresustainability.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Baltimore-City-Landscape-Manual.pdf
source: Baltimore City Department of Planning, Baltimore City Landscape Manual (adopted 2017)
---

**Key takeaway:** In Baltimore, every street tree pit must be at least **48 square feet**, and each tree needs at least **144 cubic feet** of soil. Pits should be at least 4 feet wide inside, and wider (over 6 feet) where possible. Longer, continuous planting strips are encouraged, because roots need room to grow into a healthy, large tree.

## Minimum tree pit dimensions by sidewalk width
The Landscape Manual sets pit sizes that leave enough room for people to walk:
| Available sidewalk width | Minimum pit width × length | Minimum pit area |
|---|---|---|
| 9 ft | 4 ft × 12 ft | 48 sq ft |
| 10–12 ft | 4–6 ft × 8–12 ft | 48 sq ft |
| 13 ft or more | 7 ft or more × 7–10 ft | 48 sq ft |

For the widest sidewalks, 6 to 12 inches of pavement may be kept along the curb where there is on-street parking. The same minimum widths apply to continuous landscape strips. Sidewalks must still keep an ADA-compliant walking width.

## Soil volume standards
- At least **144 cubic feet** of soil per tree (at least 48 square feet of soil area). The ideal is **1 to 2 cubic feet of soil per square foot of mature canopy**.
- Planting areas should be at least **3 feet deep** where feasible.
- Inside clear width should be at least **4 feet**, and more than **6 feet** is preferred.
- Planting areas should be as long and continuous as possible. In very urban spots, structural options can add rooting space under paving: suspended sidewalks, structural cells, soil trenches (usually at least 5 feet wide) or narrow root paths.

## Protecting the pit
- Raised curbs around pits are discouraged, because they stop runoff from nearby pavement from draining into the soil.
- In busy pedestrian areas, edges may be protected on three sides (not the curb side). Tree pit guards are low metal fences 18 to 24 inches high, with the street side left open so car doors don't hit them.
- Street trees need a permit to be planted in the public right-of-way, and new and existing pits must include soil amendments that meet city soil standards.

## Why soil volume matters
The manual says trees need enough quality soil for healthy roots and canopy, and that good soil volume also helps stormwater soak in. The ideal amount of soil grows with the size of the tree's mature canopy. The manual's preferred tree list also marks which species suit planting under utility lines.
