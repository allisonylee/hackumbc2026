title: "How much cooler? A 10% canopy increase lowers air temperature by 0.8°C (Zaerpour et al. 2025)"

source: Zaerpour, Papalexiou and Pietroniro, npj Urban Sustainability 2025

**Key takeaway:** In a heatwave study in Calgary, Canada, adding 10% more tree canopy in hot spots lowered air temperature by about 0.8°C (1.44°F), and adding 30% lowered it by up to 1.5°C (2.7°F). Tree canopy was the biggest cooling factor, explaining 67% of the differences in air temperature across the city.

## The study
Masoud Zaerpour, Simon Michael Papalexiou and Alain Pietroniro studied Calgary, Alberta, during a July 2023 heatwave. They combined a citywide network of accurate air temperature sensors with detailed satellite data. The paper was published in npj Urban Sustainability on November 17, 2025.

## Surface heat vs. the air you feel
Satellites measure how hot the ground and roofs are, not the air people breathe. The study found the satellite-based heat island was about **twice** as strong as the air-based one: **8.9°C** versus **4.6°C** on average. So maps of hot surfaces can overstate how much hotter the air actually is.

## Planting scenarios
The researchers modeled adding canopy to hot spots, meaning areas where air temperature passed Calgary's heatwave threshold of **29°C**:
- **+10% canopy:** average hot-spot temperature fell from about **31°C** to **30°C**, and **11.6%** of hot-spot areas dropped below the threshold.
- **+20% canopy:** average about **29.6°C**; **18.0%** dropped below the threshold.
- **+30% canopy:** average about **29.2°C**; **31.6%** dropped below the threshold.

Each extra step of canopy added cooling, but the gains were not perfectly equal for each step.

## Fairness
Across Calgary's **290** neighborhoods, lower-income areas (many in the east of the city) had less tree cover and were hotter during heatwaves. The authors call for targeting tree planting to vulnerable neighborhoods, alongside city heat action plans during extreme heat.

## For Baltimore
This study was not done in Baltimore, and Calgary's climate is different. Still, it gives a realistic sense of scale: increasing tree canopy by 10% in a hot area may cool the air by close to a degree Celsius.
