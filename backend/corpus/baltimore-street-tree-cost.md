**Key takeaway:** Planting a street tree where there is no tree pit is expensive. A Baltimore Tree Trust grant manager told WYPR in 2026 that cutting a new tree well into a sidewalk costs about **$1,000**, and planting and maintaining the tree costs about another **$1,000**.

## Existing pit vs. new pit
- **New tree well (cutting concrete):** about $1,000 just to cut out the sidewalk and prepare the well, then about $1,000 more to plant and maintain the tree. That is roughly $2,000 in total.
- **Existing empty pit:** the article does not give a separate figure. The planting-and-maintenance part alone is about $1,000.

## A big new project
WYPR reported that in late September 2026 Baltimore Tree Trust began site work on a two-year project to cut **900 new tree wells**, mostly in South and West Baltimore, across 18 neighborhoods. It is funded by a $900,000 grant from the Chesapeake and Atlantic Coastal Bays Trust Fund, with a total of just under $1.6 million including leveraged funds. The article also describes how crew members without experience have been trained into skilled positions.

## Why new pits matter
Many blocks in hot, low-canopy neighborhoods have no room for trees because the sidewalk is solid concrete. Cutting new wells is what makes planting possible there.

## For residents
TreeBaltimore's street tree request page (https://www.treebaltimore.org/street-tree-request-form) does not list a cost to the resident. That page also says that if you plant a street tree yourself, you need a Forestry permit, plus a Department of Transportation permit for a new pit.

To help pay for trees, you can donate to Baltimore Tree Trust: https://www.baltimoretreetrust.org/ways-to-take-action/donate/.
