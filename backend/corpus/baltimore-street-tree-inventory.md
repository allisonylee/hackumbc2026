**Key takeaway:** Baltimore's Forestry Division has mapped every street tree and park tree it is responsible for, plus empty spots where a tree could go. When finished in 2018, the inventory had over **122,000 trees** and about **40,000 vacant planting sites**.

## What the inventory is
The inventory is the city's first citywide survey of street trees and park trees. Crews collected the data over two years during the leaf-on season, finishing in July 2018.

## What it covers
- All street trees in the public street right-of-way.
- Trees in the maintained areas of city parks run by the Department of Recreation and Parks.

## What it records
The inventory shows how many trees there are, where they are, what species they are, and what condition they are in.

## Vacant planting sites
A vacant planting site is an empty spot, such as an empty tree pit along a sidewalk, where a tree could be planted. These are good starting points for new street trees because the space already exists.

## Why the city uses it
The inventory lets the Forestry Division plan strategically and shift some work from reacting to problems toward more efficient, scheduled rotations for maintenance and planting.
