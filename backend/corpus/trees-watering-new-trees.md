---
title: "How often and how much should I water a newly planted tree? (first two years)"
url: https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-trees-and-shrubs
source: University of Maryland Extension, Watering Trees and Shrubs (updated 2026)
---

**Key takeaway:** A new tree needs regular, deep watering for its **first two years**. Check the soil at least once a week, and water deeply whenever it feels dry about 6 inches down. Don't overwater: new trees can drown if they are watered too often.

## Right after planting
- Water immediately and thoroughly after planting.
- For the **first 2 weeks**, check soil moisture **every day**. Water deeply if the top 6 inches of soil feel dry.

## The first two years
- Check soil moisture **at least once a week** and water when the soil is dry at about 6 inches deep. UMD Extension says this steady care is especially important to help new roots get established.
- Evergreens should be watered deeply in the fall, before the ground freezes, if there has not been enough rain.

## How much water
- Apply **1 to 2 inches** of water, enough to wet the soil at least 6 inches deep. That is about **65 to 130 gallons per 100 square feet** of ground.
- The right amount depends on your soil type and the weather.
- Slow, deep soakings are better than frequent light sprinkles.

## How to check the soil
Dig into the soil with a trowel, small shovel or screwdriver to about 6 inches deep. If the soil is moist or damp, wait. Water only if it feels dry.

## Don't overwater
UMD Extension warns that many new trees are accidentally drowned by watering too often. Roots should never sit in standing water, which damages them.

## Mulch helps
A **2 to 3 inch** layer of mulch cuts water lost to evaporation, evens out soil temperature and reduces how often you need to water in dry spells.
