title: "Will a street tree break my pipes or sidewalk? Can I say no? (Baltimore Tree Trust FAQ)"

---

**Key takeaway:** Baltimore Tree Trust (BTT), a nonprofit that plants street trees as part of TreeBaltimore, says residents can decline a tree in front of their home, that BTT cares for new trees for their first two years, and that roots do not break pipes that are intact.

## Can I say no?
Yes. Residents can decline a tree in front of their house by filling out BTT's Tree Decline Form online, emailing info@baltimoretreetrust.org, or telling a Neighborhood Forester before installation. You can also decline concrete cutting (the Site Prep Decline Form). BTT says residents keep the final say, even though street trees stand on city property under the Baltimore City Department of Recreation and Parks.

## How will I find out?
Before a project, BTT reaches out through local organizations and community meetings, sends mail, and goes door to door, leaving information for people who aren't home.

## Can I choose the species?
Feedback is welcome, but BTT can't promise a specific tree. BTT's ISA Certified Arborists and trained Neighborhood Foresters make the final species choice from Baltimore City-approved trees, based on availability and the site.

## Who takes care of it?
BTT's Neighborhood Foresters stake, water, mulch and prune new trees for the first 2 years, and replace trees that die during that time. After that, Baltimore City Recreation and Parks is responsible for street tree maintenance. Residents can help by giving a young tree about 20 gallons of water a week from May to October.

## Will roots break my pipes?
BTT says root tips are too fragile to break an intact pipe. Roots only get into pipes that already have cracks, and the fix is to replace the damaged line with watertight PVC or cast iron, not to remove the tree.

## Will it ruin my sidewalk?
BTT's standard size for new tree wells is 4 by 8 feet, which gives roots more room, and it follows ADA standards. If a large mature tree lifts a sidewalk, contact Baltimore 311 and ask the Footways Division to repair it; the city will not remove a tree because it conflicts with a sidewalk.

## Why my neighborhood?
BTT focuses on neighborhoods with very little canopy, as low as 6%, while also replacing aging trees in greener areas. It says trees absorb heat and pollution, help with asthma, lower utility bills and add value to homes.
