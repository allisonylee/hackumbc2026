---
title: "How much electricity do data centers use? (IEA Energy and AI)"
url: https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary
source: International Energy Agency (IEA), Energy and AI, 2025
---

**Key takeaway:** Data centers used about **415 TWh** of electricity in 2024, around **1.5%** of the world's electricity. The IEA expects this to more than double to about **945 TWh** by 2030, with AI the most important driver. So one chatbot answer is tiny, but the total is large and growing.

## Today
- Data center electricity use has grown about 12% per year since 2017.
- In 2024 the United States accounted for about 45% of data center electricity use, China about 25% and Europe about 15%.

## Looking ahead
- **2030:** about 945 TWh, slightly more than all of Japan's electricity use today.
- The United States accounts for by far the largest share of this growth, followed by China. In the US, data centers make up nearly half of all electricity demand growth to 2030. By then, US data centers may use more electricity than all its heavy industries, such as aluminum, steel, cement and chemicals, combined.
- **2035:** about 1,200 TWh in the IEA's base case, with a range of about 700 to 1,700 TWh across its scenarios.

## Emissions
- Electricity use by data centers causes about **180 million tonnes** of CO2 emissions today.
- This could reach about 300 million tonnes by 2035 in the base case, or 500 million tonnes in a faster-growth case.
- That is still below 1.5% of total energy sector emissions.

## How this fits with per-query numbers
The IEA report looks at whole data centers, not single answers. A single chatbot answer uses little energy, but many answers and many other digital services, all running in data centers, add up to the large totals above.

## Caveats
These are projections with a wide range, depending on how fast AI is adopted, how efficient hardware becomes, and how quickly power supply can grow. The figures cover all data centers, not only AI.
