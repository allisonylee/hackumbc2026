**Key takeaway:** Directly under overhead wires, or within 20 feet of the line, plant only small trees that stay **25 feet tall or less** when mature. Taller trees there will grow into the lines and get pruned again and again. A simple rule of thumb: plant a tree at least as far from the wires as its expected mature height.

## The rule of thumb
The Arbor Day Foundation suggests measuring from the center line between utility poles and planting a tree at least as far away as its mature height. For example, a tree expected to reach 35 feet should be planted 35 feet from the line.

## Three zones around overhead lines
- **Low zone:** directly under the lines, or within 20 feet of the pole-to-pole center line. Small trees only, 25 feet or less at maturity. The foundation says never to plant anything taller here.
- **Medium zone:** 20 to 50 feet from the center line. Small and medium trees, up to 40 feet at maturity.
- **Tall zone:** 50 feet or more from the lines. Any size of tree, but the rule of thumb above still applies.

## Underground lines and buildings
- Stay at least **5 feet** from buried utility lines, and **10 feet or more** from sewer lines.
- Distance from buildings: small trees (25 feet or less) at least 8–10 feet from a wall or 6–8 feet from a corner; medium trees (up to 40 feet) at least 15 feet from walls and 12 feet from a corner; large trees (over 40 feet) at least 20 feet from a wall and 15 feet from a corner. The same distances are good guides for walkways and driveways.
- **Call 811 before you dig** so buried utilities can be marked.

## Why it matters
The foundation's classic "wrong tree in the wrong place" is a shade tree planted right under power lines and pruned again and again by the power company to keep the lights on. Trees and branches hitting overhead wires are the number one reason power goes out in communities. Avoiding spots that will conflict with utility lines is the number one way to help a tree reach its full potential and live a long, productive life.
