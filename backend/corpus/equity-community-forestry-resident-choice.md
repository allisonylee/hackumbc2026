**Key takeaway:** Baltimore Tree Trust (BTT) is testing a new way of planting street trees. Instead of an opt-out model, where trees are planted unless a resident declines, its Community Forestry pilot uses a resident-choice approach: neighbors decide, help plant and help care for the trees on their blocks.

## Why change the model
Under an opt-out model, the planting group chooses the sites and residents have to speak up to refuse. A 2014 study of East Baltimore found mixed feelings about street trees and recommended involving residents in decisions and caring for existing trees (see the document on asking residents). A resident-choice model puts neighbors in the role of stewards from the start.

## How the pilot works
- **First neighborhoods:** Park Circle and Pimlico Terrace.
- **Listening first:** during the summer before planting, BTT's outreach team held block-by-block meetings to learn what residents want their streets to look like.
- **Planting together:** planting was set to begin that November.
- **Training and support:** participants get hands-on training from BTT's Urban Forestry Technicians and a direct way to report maintenance problems.

## Goals
BTT is aiming for a 90% twelve-month survival rate for the pilot's trees, which it describes as a 10-point improvement over its baseline. The article says similar programs, where residents select, plant and care for trees with tools and training, have reached 95% tree survival after six years. Better survival matters because a tree only delivers shade and cooling if it lives long enough to grow.

## Background
According to the article, BTT has planted more than 22,000 trees across Baltimore since 2008. The work supports the city goal of growing tree canopy from 29% to 40%.

## Why it matters for equity
The article notes that Baltimore's tree canopy is unevenly distributed, a legacy that shapes public health today, and that neighborhoods such as Park Circle and Pimlico have fewer trees, which adds to heat islands and poorer air quality. Letting residents choose, and backing them with training and maintenance, is one way to make tree planting something done with a community rather than to it.
