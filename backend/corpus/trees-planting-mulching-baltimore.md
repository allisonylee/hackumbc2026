**Key takeaway:** Plant the tree so the trunk flare (where the trunk widens at the base) sits at ground level, not buried. Water it at least weekly through the first growing season. Spread mulch about 3 inches deep, but leave a 3-inch ring of bare soil around the trunk. Never pile mulch up against the bark.

## Digging the hole
- Make the hole at least **6 inches wider** than the container on every side, and at least **twice the container's diameter** overall.
- Dig only about as deep as the root ball, so the finished soil sits slightly high (about **½ inch**) compared with the surrounding ground.
- Baltimore has a lot of clay soil. Digging can "glaze" or smear the sides of the hole so roots and water can't get through. Rough up the bottom and sides with a garden fork, and leave the center slightly raised so water spreads out.

## Planting the tree
- Slide the tree out of its container carefully. Lift it by the root ball, never by the trunk.
- If the roots are tightly packed, gently tease out fine roots. Trim dead or crushed roots and straighten roots that circle the pot.
- Set the tree so the **trunk flare and top of the root ball are at grade** (ground level).
- Backfill with the original soil and press it down gently by hand.
- Prune only dead or injured branches at planting time.

## Watering
- Water when you plant.
- During the first growing season, water **at least once a week** when it hasn't rained, and **twice a week** in the hottest part of summer.
- Check the soil **6 to 8 inches** down. If it is powdery or crumbly, the tree needs water.
- Water deeply so moisture reaches **12 to 18 inches** below the surface. Deep soakings beat frequent light watering.

## Mulching
- Use about **3 inches** of composted material or wood chips.
- Spread it out as wide as the tree's crown.
- Keep a **3-inch circle of bare soil** around the trunk. Deep mulch, especially when piled against the trunk, can cause insect or disease problems.
