**Key takeaway:** The city's canopy page says Baltimore's existing tree canopy was measured at 27.4%, well short of the 40% canopy that American Forests recommends for healthy cities.

## What "tree canopy" means
Tree canopy is the share of the city's land that is covered by tree leaves and branches when seen from above. More canopy means more shade, cooler streets and less stormwater runoff.

## Current canopy
Baltimore City's canopy page reports the existing canopy at **27.4 percent**. That figure comes from the Maryland Department of Natural Resources and the U.S. Forest Service.

## The 40% goal
The city page says American Forests, a national nonprofit conservation group, recommends a 40% tree canopy for healthy cities. TreeBaltimore's home page (https://www.treebaltimore.org) says the city's tree-planting program works to increase Baltimore's canopy to 40%.

## Where new trees could go
The page points out where there is room for more trees:
- About 43% of the city is hard surfaces such as streets, buildings and parking lots.
- About 19% is grassland that could be planted.
- Big chances exist in rowhouse front and back yards, and on large properties such as colleges, schools, hospitals and industrial sites.

## How residents can help
The TreeBaltimore site (https://www.treebaltimore.org) lists ways to help close the gap: request a street tree, get a free yard tree, attend tree events, or become a TreeKeeper.
