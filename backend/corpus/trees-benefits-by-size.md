source: McPherson et al., USDA Forest Service, Northeast Community Tree Guide (PSW-GTR-202), 2007
---

**Key takeaway:** In the Northeast, a public street or park tree pays back more than it costs, and the bigger the mature tree, the bigger the payoff. Average yearly net benefits (benefits minus costs, averaged over 40 years) are about $9 for a small public tree, $52 for a medium one and $113 for a large one.

## Where the numbers come from
The US Forest Service guide modeled four "typical" trees, using growth data from street trees in Queens, New York City:
- **Small:** Kwanzan cherry
- **Medium:** red maple
- **Large:** Japanese zelkova
- **Conifer:** eastern white pine

The authors say these species were chosen because data existed, not as planting recommendations. The model assumes 66 percent of trees survive over 40 years. Energy savings use 2006 New York City electricity and gas prices ($0.14/kWh and $1.48/therm). So the dollar figures are roughly in 2006–2007 dollars and are first-order estimates for the whole Northeast region.

## Average yearly values over 40 years (public trees)
| | Small | Medium | Large | Conifer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benefits | $26–$30 | $69–$79 | $125–$147 | $54–$56 |
| Costs (public) | $20 | $27 | $34 | $23 |
| Net benefit (public) | $9 | $52 | $113 | $33 |

(The benefit ranges cover both yard and public trees.) Summed over 40 years, net benefits per public tree were about $364 (small), $2,066 (medium), $4,531 (large) and $1,322 (conifer).

## What a tree does 20 years after planting (public site)
- **Rain caught (interception):** 312 gal (small), 1,014 gal (medium), 1,624 gal (large) per year.
- **Electricity saved:** 16 kWh, 33 kWh and 89 kWh per year.
- **Net CO2 reduced:** about 137 lb, 242 lb and 509 lb per year.
- **Total yearly benefits:** about $28, $75 and $150.

## What the benefits are made of
In the guide's example city, higher property values (44 percent) and energy savings (34 percent) made up most of the value. Less stormwater runoff (11 percent), cleaner air (9 percent) and lower CO2 (1 percent) made up the rest. Planting is the largest single cost for public trees (about $10–$15 per tree per year when spread over 40 years), followed by pruning.
