**Key takeaway:** Baltimore's 2018 street tree list gives rough mature sizes in feet. Small trees such as eastern redbud and serviceberry stay around 20 to 30 feet tall. Big shade trees such as London planetree and Kentucky coffeetree can pass 70 feet. This list is older: the city's 2025 Approved Tree Species List (updated 8/29/2025) replaced it and changed some advice (see notes below).

## Mature size (spread / height, in feet), 2018 list
**Small trees for under power lines**
- Eastern redbud (Cercis canadensis): spread 15–25, height 20–30. Native. 2018 comment: overplanted and short-lived, so consider alternatives.
- Shadblow serviceberry (Amelanchier canadensis): spread 15–20, height 20–25. Native. Starred as low or multi-limbed, so only for wide open areas unless trained to a single trunk.
- Hedge maple (Acer campestre): spread 30–35, height 25–30. Exotic. **Note:** the 2025 Approved Tree Species List bans it as invasive.

**Medium and large trees**
- American hornbeam (Carpinus caroliniana): 20–35 / 35–50. Native.
- American yellowwood (Cladrastis kentukea): 25–35 / 25–40. Listed native in 2018.
- Blackgum / tupelo (Nyssa sylvatica): 30–40 / 30–50. Native.
- American hophornbeam (Ostrya virginiana): 25–30 / 30–40. Native.
- Red maple (Acer rubrum): 25–35 / 60–75. Native. 2018 comment: overplanted, so consider alternatives.
- Kentucky coffeetree, male (Gymnocladus dioicus): 40–50 / 70–80. Native. Avoid females, which drop beans.
- London planetree (Platanus × acerifolia): 50–70 / 70–85.
- Swamp white oak (Quercus bicolor): 50–70 / 50–70. Native. Comment: establishes well.
- Willow oak (Quercus phellos): 40–50 / 50–75. Native.

## What "native" meant on the 2018 list
The 2018 list calls native a regional term, meaning the tree grew in Maryland even if not originally in Baltimore itself (red maple is its example). The 2025 list defines native as historically present in the Mid-Atlantic, and it relabels yellowwood as native to the Southeast.

## General advice on the 2018 list
Native species give more benefits, and in larger plantings at least half should be native. Large shade trees should go wherever there is room. The list also says that care over several years (weeding, mulching, straightening) is key, and that routine watering is the most important thing you can do for a new tree.
